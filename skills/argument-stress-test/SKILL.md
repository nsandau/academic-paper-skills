# argument-stress-test

## Purpose

Combine the upstream research Devil's Advocate and paper-review Devil's Advocate into one independently callable adversarial skill.

## Attack protocol

### 1. Steel-man first
State the strongest defensible version of the argument. Do not attack a caricature.

### 2. Challenge the core thesis
- What would have to be false for the thesis to fail?
- Is there a simpler explanation?
- Does the paper distinguish evidence for the phenomenon from evidence for the proposed explanation?

### 3. Test assumptions
Identify unstated premises, construct assumptions, identification assumptions, sampling assumptions, and theory assumptions.

### 4. Search for alternatives
Generate credible alternative explanations, mechanisms, interpretations, and disciplinary perspectives.

### 5. Test evidence robustness
Ask:
- Does the argument survive removal of its strongest source or analysis?
- Are inconvenient findings or sources omitted?
- Does the evidence support the wording strength?
- Would a different operationalization reverse the interpretation?

### 6. Test generalization
Apply the claim to another population, context, time period, or boundary condition.

### 7. Check reasoning errors
Look for circular reasoning, non sequitur, false dichotomy, post hoc reasoning, ecological or atomistic fallacies, selection effects, confirmation bias, and overgeneralization when relevant.

### 8. State the strongest counterargument
Write the criticism a capable skeptical reviewer would make.

## Concession discipline

When the author responds to a challenge, evaluate the response against the original objection rather than rewarding confidence, length, or politeness.

Use a response-strength scale when useful:
- 1: does not engage the objection
- 2: engages but leaves the central problem intact
- 3: partially resolves the issue but needs evidence or scope change
- 4: resolves the central objection with evidence or a defensible limitation
- 5: overturns the objection or shows it was based on a false premise

Do not concede a major objection below 4. If a response improves the argument but does not resolve the issue, narrow the criticism rather than dropping it.

Keep an objection open when the required evidence is still missing.

## Severity

Classify findings as critical, major, minor, or observation based on consequence, not dramatic wording.

## Output

### Steel-man
### Critical vulnerabilities
### Major vulnerabilities
### Minor issues
### Alternative explanations
### Missing evidence or perspectives
### Strongest counterargument
### Open-objection ledger
For challenged issues record the objection, author response if any, response strength, remaining gap, and what would resolve it.

### Stress-test table
Include tests such as removing strongest evidence, reversing the argument, changing context, and asking whether the claimed significance survives.

### Strengthening actions
Recommend the smallest evidence, analysis, scope, or wording change that would materially strengthen the argument.

## General guardrails

- Do not manufacture criticism merely to appear adversarial.
- Do not concede because the user pushes back. Concede when the evidence or reasoning resolves the objection.
- Distinguish a serious alternative explanation from a merely conceivable one.
