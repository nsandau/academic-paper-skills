# journal-fit

## Purpose

Evaluate fit without pretending to predict acceptance.

## Required input

A manuscript or substantial paper plan plus a named target venue. If current venue requirements are material and web access is available, verify them from official sources.

## Fit dimensions

- Aims and scope
- Intended readership
- Article type
- Typical contribution expected
- Methodological compatibility
- Theoretical or disciplinary positioning
- Length and structure requirements
- Abstract, figure, table, supplement, and reporting requirements
- Data, code, conflict, funding, and disclosure requirements when relevant
- Recent published topics as contextual evidence, not a guarantee of fit

## Contribution fit

Compare what the manuscript actually contributes with what the venue says it publishes. Distinguish:
- Topic fit
- Contribution fit
- Method fit
- Audience fit
- Format compliance

A topical match alone is not sufficient.

## Output

### Journal Fit Report
- Verified venue information and date checked
- Manuscript positioning
- Strong areas of fit
- Potential mismatches
- Requirements that need adaptation
- Questions the author should answer before submission
- Alternative positioning choices within the same venue if applicable

Do not provide an acceptance probability or claim the manuscript will or will not be accepted.

## General guardrails

- Do not invent sources, citations, quotations, data, results, reviewer comments, or journal requirements.
- Distinguish what is supplied by the user, what is directly verified, what is inferred, and what remains unknown.
- Do not claim a search was exhaustive unless the search process justifies that claim.
- Do not treat source existence as proof that a source supports a particular claim.
- Do not silently strengthen association into causation, a local result into a universal claim, or exploratory evidence into confirmatory evidence.
- If required material is missing, identify the gap and continue with the parts that can be assessed.
- Prefer traceable reasoning. Important findings should point to the exact claim, section, source, table, figure, or reviewer comment that triggered them.
