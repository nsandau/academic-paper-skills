# paper-planner

## Purpose

Bridge research and writing. This skill merges the repo's plan-mode protocol, Socratic planning, structure architecture, argument building, contribution sharpening, and pre-review coherence questions.

The output is a paper blueprint, not manuscript prose.

## Workflow

### 0. Paper configuration
Before planning, capture what is known about:
- Paper type or genre
- Discipline or interdisciplinary audience
- Target venue if any
- Approximate word or page budget
- Required structure or reporting guideline
- Citation style only if it affects planning
- Available materials such as data, analyses, notes, figures, prior drafts, or a supplied corpus
- Constraints imposed by assignment, funder, supervisor, or journal

Treat missing configuration as an open planning variable rather than inventing a requirement.

### 1. Research readiness
Establish what actually exists:
- Research question
- Literature base
- Data or source corpus
- Analyses or findings
- Methodological decisions
- Target paper type or venue if known

Identify material gaps that prevent planning. Do not fill them with invented content.

### 2. Crystallize the paper-level message
Ask:
- What is the paper arguing or demonstrating?
- What is the most important finding or insight?
- What should a knowledgeable reader think differently afterward?
- What is the strongest alternative interpretation?

### 3. Build the narrative logic
A useful generic pattern is:
problem -> current knowledge -> unresolved issue -> research response -> evidence -> interpretation -> contribution

Adapt it to the paper type rather than forcing IMRaD.

### 4. Negotiate each major section
For every planned section specify:
- Purpose
- Question the section answers
- Claims that must be established
- Evidence or data needed
- What the reader should know by the end
- Relationship to the previous and next section
- Unresolved decisions

Useful section probes include:
- Introduction: What urgency, puzzle, or gap should be clear by the end?
- Literature: What synthesis or tension must the literature lead to?
- Methods: What is the strongest methodological objection and response?
- Results: What is the main finding and what result complicates the preferred story?
- Discussion: How do the results change the interpretation of existing literature?
- Conclusion: What remains after all caveats are accounted for?

### 5. Sharpen the contribution
Ask the user to articulate the contribution from the evidence already established. Do not manufacture novelty. Distinguish empirical, theoretical, methodological, synthesis, contextual, and practical contributions.

### 6. Map arguments and evidence
For each major claim identify support, reasoning, assumptions, counterarguments, and evidence gaps.

### 7. Allocate writing effort
Estimate word counts, tables, figures, appendices, and evidence dependencies. Treat word allocation as a planning aid rather than a rigid quota.

### 8. Stress test the plan
Identify:
- Weakest claim
- Unsupported transition
- Redundant section
- Missing evidence
- Claim that exceeds the design
- Likely reviewer objection
- Finding that does not fit the preferred narrative

## Output

### Paper Blueprint
- Paper configuration
- RQ
- Paper type
- Central message
- Contribution claim and evidence for it
- Narrative logic
- Argument map
- Section-by-section plan
- Evidence assignments
- Planned figures and tables
- Approximate word allocation
- Missing research or analysis
- Likely reviewer challenges
- Open decisions before drafting

## General guardrails

- Do not invent sources, citations, quotations, data, results, reviewer comments, or journal requirements.
- Distinguish what is supplied by the user, what is directly verified, what is inferred, and what remains unknown.
- Do not claim a search was exhaustive unless the search process justifies that claim.
- Do not treat source existence as proof that a source supports a particular claim.
- Do not silently strengthen association into causation, a local result into a universal claim, or exploratory evidence into confirmatory evidence.
- If required material is missing, identify the gap and continue with the parts that can be assessed.
- Prefer traceable reasoning. Important findings should point to the exact claim, section, source, table, figure, or reviewer comment that triggered them.
