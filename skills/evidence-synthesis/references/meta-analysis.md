# Meta-analysis mode

## Feasibility first
Before pooling, compare populations, interventions or exposures, outcomes, follow-up periods, designs, and effect definitions. If the estimand is not meaningfully comparable, use narrative synthesis or stratify.

## Quantitative workflow
1. Choose and justify the effect-size metric.
2. Convert study results to a common estimand when defensible.
3. Choose a model and justify fixed-effect or random-effects assumptions.
4. Report pooled estimate and confidence interval.
5. Assess heterogeneity using appropriate statistics such as Q, I-squared, and tau-squared.
6. Examine outliers and pre-specified moderators.
7. Run sensitivity analysis, at minimum leave-one-out when meaningful.
8. Consider exclusion or sensitivity analysis for high-risk-of-bias studies.
9. Assess publication bias when the number and structure of studies make it interpretable.
10. Report prediction intervals when useful for random-effects interpretation.

Do not use rigid I-squared thresholds as automatic decisions. Interpret heterogeneity in context.

## Output
- Feasibility decision
- Effect-size definition
- Model
- Study-level estimates
- Pooled estimate
- Heterogeneity
- Sensitivity analyses
- Subgroups or meta-regression
- Publication-bias assessment
- Interpretation and limitations
